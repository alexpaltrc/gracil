# Changelog

What's new in each version of Gracil.

## 1.0 (in development)

Gracil's first public version, still being finished. You can already compress videos and images
with it.

### New

- Gracil is free and open source: its code is public on GitHub for anyone to read, use and share
  under the GNU General Public License, and the licenses of everything inside it come with it.
  There is nothing to install. Unpack its zip wherever you like, such as your Documents or a USB
  drive, and open Gracil. Everything it needs comes inside, so it never uses the internet, and it
  keeps its settings and its diary in its own folder and nowhere else, so they go wherever Gracil
  goes. On a drive it can't write to, it still compresses, it just doesn't remember your
  settings. To remove Gracil, delete its folder: nothing else is left. To leave Windows exactly
  as it was, first choose **Prepare to delete Gracil** in the right-click menu of its marble next
  to the clock: Gracil closes and shows you its folder, ready to delete.
- Gracil is in English and Spanish. It uses the language Windows is shown in, so there is nothing
  to set. Numbers are written the way Windows writes them for your region, for example
  **15,031 files** and **1.2 GB**.
- Gracil wears Windows 11's own colors, in light and dark, so it looks like part of Windows. Its
  buttons and progress take the accent color you picked in Windows. It follows Windows' light or
  dark mode and your accent color, and when you change either, Gracil fades gently to match.
  Every word and button stays easy to read in both, even on a pale accent color.
- Gracil follows Windows' contrast themes, drawing with their colors alone, and Windows'
  **Animation effects**: when they are off, nothing in Gracil moves. In a contrast theme, buttons
  light up in the theme's highlight color when you point at them or reach them with the keyboard,
  and buttons you can't use yet are clearly dimmed.
- When the window changes what it shows, for example from the work to the finished view, the new
  view fades in softly instead of jumping.
- Gracil has its own icon, a marble resting on a support, in the window, the taskbar and File
  Explorer. It sits on a small tile in Windows' own greys, always the opposite of Windows: dark
  when Windows is light, light when Windows is dark, so it stands out in both. It stays sharp at
  any screen size.
- Drop videos, images or whole folders onto the window, or choose files, and Gracil compresses them.
  The empty window shows where to drop and, at its foot, the **Always** line; everything else comes
  up once there are files. You can keep adding files while it works, by dropping them or with the
  **+** at the bottom of the window.
- Gracil works on several files at once: while one video compresses, the next one gets ready, so
  there is no wait between them, and images compress a few at a time alongside it. Two videos are
  never compressed at the same time.
- Gracil never gets in your way: it works below your other programs, and Windows itself keeps it
  to part of the processor, half while you use the computer, never more than half on a laptop and
  a quarter on battery, so the
  computer stays responsive, cool and quiet, even a simple laptop. It never fills your drive or
  your memory. While you use the computer, and always on battery, Windows runs Gracil's work at
  the processor's calmest speed, so the computer stays cooler and quieter. It takes on more when
  you leave the computer alone and less as soon as you come back, and fewer large images at once
  so memory never runs short. The window stays quick to answer even after hours of work on tens
  of thousands of files.
- While a film, a game, a presentation or a call is on, Gracil takes as little of the computer as
  while you are using it. With nothing to do, it no longer checks the computer every second.
- On battery below 30 %, the files under way finish and no new one starts; below 15 %, everything
  waits, saying **Low battery · waiting**. Plugging in carries on by itself.
- A video interrupted by the computer going to sleep is no longer left uncompressed: Gracil waits
  for the computer to wake up and tries again.
- Videos larger than 4K have their quality checked with much less memory, and several files
  compressing onto the same nearly full drive leave room for each other.
- Gracil's window opens even on computers whose graphics card can't draw it.
- Images on a network or cloud drive compress much faster: each one is read from the drive only
  once, and the next few are read while others compress.
- Stop stops the whole list at once, even on a slow network or cloud drive. Nothing is lost: the
  files not done yet wait on a quiet line with **Continue**, so you can carry on whenever you
  like. Exit no longer keeps you waiting either.
- With tens of thousands of files on the list, the window stays as smooth as with a few, and
  **Clear** and **Stop** answer at once.
- Videos spend less time getting ready, above all on computers with an NVIDIA graphics card, and
  leave the processor freer meanwhile. The result is exactly the same as before. In a list of
  similar videos, such as those from one phone, each one after the first gets ready quicker
  still.
- On a desktop computer, a few more photos compress at once while you use it. Laptops are
  unchanged.
- Intel and AMD graphics now read the videos too, not just compress them, when a quick test at
  start shows they can, so the processor stays freer. Otherwise nothing changes.
- Gracil never lets the computer run too hot, and it never has to stop to cool down. It keeps
  the graphics card at about 70 °C at most, whether it is NVIDIA, Intel or AMD, as long as the
  card tells Windows its temperature: above that it eases off little by
  little, with pauses too short to notice, until it finds the pace that holds the temperature
  there, instead of slowing down sharply and speeding back up. The work only goes a little
  slower.
- If Gracil closes before its list is done, because you chose **Exit**, it stopped suddenly or the
  computer was turned off, it remembers the files left. Next time, a quiet line offers
  **Continue** to carry on with them.
- Gracil runs only once. Opening it again, from its folder, the taskbar or anywhere else, brings
  the open Gracil's window forward, even when it was closed next to the clock. Opening videos with
  it while it is running adds them to the same list.
- Closing the window no longer stops Gracil: it keeps working next to the clock. Its marble there
  fills up as the work goes on, shows two bars when paused, and changes to Windows' warning color if
  something went wrong while the window was closed; click it then, and the small window opens on the
  file that went wrong. Right-click it for **Open Gracil**, Gracil's settings, **Prepare to delete
  Gracil** and **Exit**. The window's name in the taskbar says how the work goes too, for example
  "Gracil · compressing 42%". If a file is compressing, waiting, or finished and waiting for your
  choice, Exit first asks, and tells you exactly what would happen.
- Click the marble next to the clock and a small window opens at once beside it, wherever your
  taskbar is. It shows the file being compressed and how far along it is, and its ring pauses and
  resumes (stopping is left to the big window). It also says how many more files are waiting and
  where a finished one went, and you can drop more files on it. When a file is done, you can choose
  **Replace** or **Save copy** right there, without opening the big window. It stays open while you
  do other things until you close it or click the marble again; **Open Gracil** in it brings up the
  big window.
- Right-click videos, images or whole folders in File Explorer, choose **Show more options**, then
  **Send to** and **Gracil**. It is there from the first time you open Gracil, with nothing to set
  up. For a folder, Gracil compresses every video and image inside it, subfolders included. What
  happens follows the **Always** line of Gracil's window: on **Replace** or **Save copy**, the files
  are compressed without opening the big window and put where the line says; on **Ask**, Gracil's
  window opens and asks after each file. Other files picked along are left alone. The small window
  opens by itself so you can watch; if you'd rather it didn't, untick **Show mini-window when
  compressing from File Explorer** in the marble's right-click menu. Gracil looks through folders in
  the background, so it keeps responding even with large folders or folders on a network or cloud
  drive: files join the list folder by folder and compressing starts with the first ones found.
  Don't want Gracil in **Send to**? Untick **Show Gracil in Send to** in the marble's right-click
  menu. After moving Gracil's folder, open Gracil once and **Send to** follows it.
- Gracil can also have its own commands in File Explorer's right-click menu: tick **Show
  Gracil in File Explorer's menu…** in the marble's right-click menu, and allow it when
  Windows asks, since the commands are for everyone who uses the computer. Videos and images then
  show **Compress with Gracil**, and a folder shows **Gracil**, to compress its videos and images,
  only its videos, or only its images. They follow the **Always** line just like **Send to**.
- Gracil starts by itself when you sign in, waiting quietly next to the clock until you give it
  videos. To stop that, untick **Start with Windows** in the marble's right-click menu. Once
  Gracil's folder is deleted, Windows forgets to start it by itself.
- Gracil compresses photos and other images too, the same way as videos: drop them in, then
  **Replace** or **Save copy**. A PNG stays a PNG; JPEG, HEIC from an iPhone, WebP, AVIF, TIFF and
  BMP become a JPEG, which opens everywhere, or a PNG when the image has see-through parts, HEIC
  and AVIF included. Every JPEG is made with jpegli, Google's modern photo compressor, which
  comes inside Gracil. The first time, a photo looks the same as before and keeps its full size,
  its date, camera, place, captions, colors and which way is up. Nothing pops up while Gracil
  works on images.
- Moving images (such as GIF), camera RAW files, JPEG XL and photos stored in HDR aren't
  compressed: they are left exactly as they are, and a small "!" says why.
- When a result can't be put in place, the "!" says why and what to do, for example **Another
  program is using this file. Close it, then choose again**, or that the file is no longer where it
  was. While Gracil gets ready, the marble next to the clock says so: **Gracil · getting ready**.
- The video being compressed is shown large, inside a ring: a marble rolls around it as the work
  advances, filling an invisible circle with color behind it. The middle of the ring holds the
  controls, as symbols instead of words: the top part pauses (II) and turns into play (▶) to resume,
  and the bottom part stops (■); pointing at it shows the words **Stop all**. Pause acts on
  everything being worked on at once. While Gracil gets ready, before the marble starts moving, the
  marble's middle gently fades and returns, so you can tell it is working. Pausing frees up your
  computer while keeping the work done so far; resuming carries on from the same point. **View
  files**, which unfolds gently and quickly even with thousands of files, shows every other video
  and image, how much smaller each one became and where it went, for example **−35% · Replaced**.
  With two or more done, it adds them all up, for example **In all: 3.4 GB → 2.2 GB, 35% smaller**,
  and the line with **View files** says how many files are left, counting down to none as they
  finish, for example **12 files left**. Click a file to show it large; click it again, close **View
  files**, or click the small line naming the file being compressed to go back to the ring. Take a
  file off the list with **×** or **Clear**. Right after **Clear**, **Undo** puts the whole list
  back, finished results included. A video can always be compressed again, even right
  after replacing it, without clearing the list first.
- With a longer list, Gracil says about how long is left, for example **12 files left, about
  20 min**, and when you point at its marble next to the clock, **Gracil · compressing 42% · about
  20 min left**. It is a calm, rounded guide that comes down as the work goes on and doesn't creep
  up, and it steps aside while you pause, while Gracil rests and near the end.
- Everything works from the keyboard too: **Tab** moves a ring from one button to the next,
  including pause and stop on the ring, and **Enter** or **Space** presses it. The button with the
  ring lights up and shows its reminder, and buttons you can't use yet are skipped. On the
  **Always** line, the arrow keys move the choice. With no ring, **Space** pauses and resumes,
  **Esc** steps back, **Ctrl+O** chooses files and **Ctrl+Z** undoes **Clear**.
- With more than one file, the ring stands for the whole list, with one equal part per file. Above
  it Gracil says how many files it holds, for example **Compressing 24 files**, and the
  percentage below is the whole list's. Adding files while Gracil works moves the marble back a
  little, since each file's part gets smaller, and the ring only closes at the real end of the
  list. The marble next to the clock follows the same ring.
- When the whole list is done, the window says so clearly: the ring's middle fills with green and a
  check mark draws itself, and just below, in large letters, how much room the list saved, for
  example **130 MB smaller**, with a quiet line such as **22 files compressed · 4 were already
  compressed**. If nothing came out smaller, or **Stop** set files aside, there is no check mark;
  when nothing came out smaller it says **Nothing to make smaller** or **Nothing was compressed**
  instead. Files that couldn't be compressed are counted apart, with a "!": click it to see the
  first of them and what happened, and again for the next one; a quiet line under the count says
  why they failed, or the most frequent reason. The small window next to the clock shows the same
  once the list is done. Below it, **Clear the list** takes you back to the empty window,
  ready for your next files.
- When a video is done, you see how much smaller it became, and its size before and after, for
  example **120 MB → 78 MB**. Then you choose: **Replace** puts it in place of the original,
  keeping its name and the date it was created; **Save copy** keeps your original and saves
  the smaller one next to it as `Gracil-` followed by its name. With several files
  done, **Replace all** and **Save all as copies** handle them in one go.
- If you always answer the same way, the line at the foot of the window lets you say so once:
  **Always**, then **Replace**, **Ask** and **Save copy**; point at **Always** for a reminder.
  Pick either end and Gracil does that with every file that finishes from then on. On **Ask**, in
  the middle, where it starts, Gracil asks each time.
- Replacing is done safely: if anything goes wrong, your original is put back. No other file is
  ever overwritten.
- If Gracil is closed suddenly, for example when the computer loses power or it is forced to
  quit, the compression it was doing stops with it instead of carrying on unseen. Gracil tidies
  away what it left half done the next time it starts. If it was replacing a video at that
  moment, it puts your original back. It only ever removes files it made itself.
- If you take a finished file off the list without choosing, the smaller version is deleted and
  your original stays as it was. If you exit first, Gracil asks, and the file is compressed again
  when you carry on next time.
- Every compressed video is an MP4, so it plays everywhere: on Windows, iPhone, Mac, Android, TVs
  and in web browsers. When that changes a video's name, Gracil shows it before you choose, for
  example `IMG_0042.MOV → IMG_0042.mp4`.
- Compressed videos keep their recording date, orientation, chapters, subtitles, audio languages
  and cover art. The few things an MP4 can't hold, such as subtitles that are pictures rather
  than text, are left out, and the video is still kept.
- With a graphics card that can compress video, Gracil uses it to compress much faster, with the
  card's quality features that make videos smaller without making them look worse. That can be a
  recent NVIDIA card, Intel graphics (including the graphics built into most Intel processors) or
  AMD graphics. Gracil checks with a tiny test which one works, and without one it uses the
  processor.
- Each video gets its own amount of compression. Gracil first tries a few short moments of the video
  and picks the strongest compression that still looks the same as the original, so easy videos come
  out much smaller and demanding ones keep their detail. This takes a few seconds to a minute while
  Gracil is getting ready, and you can stop it at any time. It looks at every other picture, which
  is just as reliable and quicker, and leaves the processor freer.
- Gracil makes videos lighter, never heavier. Those same short moments tell it how big the video
  will come out, so it only compresses when the result will be clearly smaller, instead of working
  for minutes to find out it isn't.
- Videos that are already compressed, like most web downloads or a video Gracil already
  compressed, are compressed a little harder on their own, without asking. They may look a little
  less sharp than the first time, which is what compressing a video again asks for. If not even
  that would make the video clearly lighter, it stays as it is and shows **Already compressed**
  with a small "!" saying it's already about as small as it can get.
- Colors are stored more finely, so skies and shadows stay smooth instead of banded.
- Every video keeps its picture size, 4K included. Videos with more than 30 frames per second
  become 30; slower ones keep exactly the rate they had. Slow motion recorded by a phone loses its
  slowed-down effect.
- Sound that takes much more room than it needs, such as uncompressed sound from cameras, is made
  compact and still sounds the same for normal listening.
- Videos from recent iPhones compress too: their spatial sound, which FFmpeg can't read yet, is
  left out, and the usual sound every player uses is kept.
- If a video is unusual, Gracil quietly tries simpler ways until one works, instead of giving up.
- If a file can't be made smaller, Gracil doesn't keep it. It says **Already compressed**, with
  no buttons, and says why just below.
- Gracil comes with FFmpeg, the free tool it uses to compress video, so there is nothing else to
  download. If that part of Gracil ever goes missing, the empty window says so right away and
  files say **Can't start** with a "!" explaining it: download Gracil again from where you got it
  and it works as before.
- If a file changes while Gracil compresses it, Gracil leaves the file as it is now and lets the
  compressed version go, with a "!" saying so. Add it again to compress the new version.
- A read-only file is never replaced, and Gracil never takes the mark off: **Replace** is turned
  off for it, with a "!" saying why, and **Save copy** still works.
- Gracil checks that it can save files in a folder before compressing, so a folder that doesn't
  take new files is found out at once, not after the whole compression.
- If FFmpeg stops answering, for example on a damaged file or a stalled network drive, Gracil
  stops it after a few minutes and tries another way, instead of waiting forever.
- Finished files are put in place one at a time, in the order you chose them, and files you let
  go are deleted in the background, so the window never waits on a slow drive.
- Folders bring in only your own files: hidden and system files, the Recycle Bin and the extra
  files a Mac leaves on USB drives are left out.
- A file kept only online by OneDrive or a similar drive needs room for its download too, and
  once Gracil is done with it, the cloud drive may free up that room again.
- Shutting down, restarting or signing out of Windows is never held up by Gracil, and the files
  left unfinished are remembered for next time.
- Opened straight from inside its zip, Gracil asks you to extract it to a folder first, instead
  of saying its video tool is missing.
- Files with very long names compress and save like any other, even on older USB drives.
- Gracil's diary of what it did stops growing at about 20 MB a day.
- Images waiting their turn never crowd your computer's memory.
- The marble next to the clock, and the window's name in the taskbar, which screen readers read,
  now say how things are in every moment: for example **Gracil · 2 files waiting for you**,
  **Gracil · low battery, waiting**, **Gracil · downloading** or **Gracil · done, 130 MB
  smaller**. When something went wrong, it says **Gracil · click to see what happened**, and the
  marble keeps its warning color until you do.
- Opened from inside its zip, Gracil says so in the empty window from the start.
- A file kept only online says **Downloading** while it comes down.
- Choosing **Exit** while Gracil is still looking through folders now asks first, and those folders
  are kept for next time with the other unfinished files.
- Pointing at a file's name shows its whole path.
- Clearer words in several places, such as **3 more to compress**, **Looking for videos and
  images…** and, in Spanish, **Vaciar la lista**.
- Gracil's window opens a little sooner, and Gracil waiting next to the clock takes less from the
  computer. On a laptop with two graphics cards, the window no longer wakes the larger one, and it
  draws less often while only the ring moves.
